The Name My Mother Keeps Repeating

A Novel of Memory, Silence and Late Love

What do you do when the only person who knows the truth is losing the words to tell it?

Silvia Belandi, 51, is a family-court judge in Bologna and a widow who believes that if everything stays in its place, nothing can hurt you. Then her mother, Anese, begins to set one plate too many at the table, to count out loud to eleven, and to say a name no one in the family has ever spoken: Lorenzo. Silvia answers the way she answers everything, with spreadsheets, deadlines and a petition for guardianship.

Dario Valenti, a geriatrician who carries a mistake of his own, tells her to stop correcting her mother and start writing her words down. Piece by piece, in a green notebook, the words add up to a nun, a grave in the chestnut woods above Bologna, and a scrap of cloth marked with a date in 1967. Everyone who could explain stays silent, and each of them does it out of love. Silvia has spent her career making people answer. Now she has to learn to listen, while the doctor she should not lean on becomes the first person in four years she wants beside her.

Her mother has fewer words every month.

Some silences protect no one. They only decide who hears the truth too late. Will Silvia hear it in time?
