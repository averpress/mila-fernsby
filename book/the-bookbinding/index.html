About the book

Some silences are kept out of love. How long before they become a wound?

Nora Fenn is a book restorer in Vienna, a woman who mends other people's broken things and opens nothing of her own. Her mother Helena has said "Good" instead of tenderness for forty years. When Helena dies, Nora is asked to repair her prayer book. "Only the spine," her mother always said. "Never open the cover."

Under the lining of the back cover Nora finds a letter dated 12 November 1947, addressed to "my child". Nora is the younger of Helena's two daughters. The letter is not for either of them.

Her search leads through a lawyer's cellar on Singerstrasse, a sister who has secrets of her own, and a quiet attorney bound by his oath to say nothing. It also leads back to the winter of 1947, when a young apprentice and a British officer met over three soaked books in a freezing workshop. Nora must choose between the family's careful silence and the truth, and she does not yet know what each will cost her.

Some covers should stay shut. What if opening this one is the only way home?
