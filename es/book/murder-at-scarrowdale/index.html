Twenty-four guests, one real body and a wildfire burning over the only way out.

Ailsa Kerr used to be head writer on Britain's most-watched crime drama. Now she scripts murder-mystery weekends for Foxglove Events, and her latest client has booked a second one: a Cold War bunker two floors under a Northumberland moor, owned by his investor. Her Inspector is Desmond Hale, the actor who played Harlow for six years, the only witness in her lawsuit over who created the show, and, since December, the man she is trying very hard not to be seen with. It is thirty degrees on the surface and twelve below it, and everyone is asking for a jumper.

On Friday evening the fire reaches the road. The fire service orders the blast doors shut and tells everyone to stay put. By Saturday morning the investor is dead, lying exactly where the game had chalked its pretend corpse, and the guests' wristbands swear that everyone but Ailsa, Desmond and their cast was asleep at the time. The dead man's briefcase holds a folder with their names on it. The generator has about sixty hours of diesel. If the game stops, the weekend, the client and the company go down with it. If it carries on, the killer is passing the sandwiches.

The wind turns on Sunday morning. Will the killer still be down there when the doors open?
