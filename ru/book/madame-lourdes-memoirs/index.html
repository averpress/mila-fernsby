A Novel of Porto, Music and Family Secrets

How much does a lifetime of silence cost, and who has been paying for it?

Inês Tavares is twenty-nine, newly out of work and a few weeks from losing her rented room in Porto. The job that could save her pays €1,200 a month: to write down the memoirs of Madame Lurdes, a ninety-one-year-old opera singer who is sharp, funny and in no hurry to explain herself. The sessions take place in a narrow house above the Douro, and the first person Inês meets there is Ricardo, Lurdes's grandson, a cooper who guards his grandmother with a grumble and tells Inês no before she has finished her first question.

Inês has made a life of leaving first. Yet Tuesday by Tuesday and Friday by Friday, between the smell of charred oak, a lemon tree in the courtyard and a piano nobody is allowed to close, she finds herself staying for dinner. Lurdes tells her about Porto in 1954 and the five years that followed, about a rehearsal room under a cold roof and the pianist who sat beside her. She tells it in her own order, and she skips the one year that matters. Meanwhile, every 24 June for decades, a letter has arrived at the house. Every one of them ends with the same four words: The door is open.

A publisher is waiting for a book. A daughter wants the questions stopped. And Inês, who has never had a story of her own to tell, must decide whose silence she is willing to protect, and what it will cost her if she is wrong.

Some secrets are kept out of love. When does the keeping turn into a locked door?
